# Chapter 3

## The Place Without Time

The tiny circle floated in front of Vivin.

It was no bigger than a coin.

He stared at it.

The machine behind him continued to spin, but the sound seemed farther away with every passing second.

The circle grew.

Coin.

Plate.

Window.

Door.

On the other side, he could hear waves.

A child's voice broke the silence.

"You came back."

Vivin's heart raced.

He looked behind him.

The workshop was still there.

The lathe was still running.

Nothing was stopping him from turning around and walking away.

He took one deep breath.

Then he stepped forward.

---

The sound disappeared.

Warm sand welcomed his feet.

The breeze carried the smell of the sea.

The beach looked exactly the same.

Only one thing had changed.

The child was gone.

A teenage boy stood where the child had been.

He looked around sixteen.

He wasn't surprised to see Vivin.

Instead...

He smiled.

"I've been waiting for you."

Vivin frowned.

"Waiting? We met for two seconds."

The boy shook his head.

"Not for me."

"What does that even mean?"

"You'll understand."

Vivin looked around.

"Where am I?"

The teenager shrugged.

"I don't know if this place has a name."

"There has to be one."

"Maybe."

"But nobody here uses it."

The answer only made things more confusing.

---

They began walking along the beach.

The sand felt warm.

The waves rolled in gently before disappearing without leaving foam behind.

Everything looked familiar.

Almost too familiar.

There were people everywhere.

Some sat quietly watching the sea.

Some laughed together.

Some walked alone.

Nobody looked worried.

Nobody seemed busy.

Nobody looked lost.

Yet nobody seemed eager to leave.

---

After a while, Vivin noticed something strange.

There were no footprints.

He looked behind him.

The sand was untouched.

He stopped walking.

The teenager stopped too.

"Our footprints..."

"There aren't any."

The teenager looked down.

"Oh."

"I never noticed."

"You never noticed?"

He smiled.

"I've stopped looking."

A chill ran through Vivin.

---

Further ahead stood a small wooden building overlooking the water.

Music drifted softly through its open windows.

People walked in.

People walked out.

No one carried money.

No one waited in line.

The teenager held the door open.

"You'll like this place."

Inside, everyone sat quietly.

Some talked.

Some simply smiled at one another.

Behind the counter stood an old bartender polishing glasses.

He looked at Vivin for only a second.

Without asking a single question...

He reached beneath the counter.

Placed a chilled beer glass on the table.

Then slowly poured an Appy Fizz into it.

The bubbles rose gently to the top.

Vivin stared at it.

"How..."

The bartender simply smiled and walked away.

The teenager picked up his own drink.

"I thought your favourite drink changed."

Vivin looked at him.

"...How do you know that's my favourite?"

The teenager took a sip.

"I don't remember."

"I just know."

---

They stepped back outside.

Vivin looked around.

"There aren't any clocks."

"No."

"What time is it?"

The teenager tilted his head.

"What is... time?"

"The hour."

"The minute."

"The day."

The teenager laughed softly.

"I don't know those words."

Vivin looked toward the sky.

The sun wasn't moving.

Neither were the shadows.

It wasn't morning.

It wasn't evening.

It simply...

...was.

---

They continued walking.

On a nearby bench, an elderly woman laughed while teaching a little girl how to fold paper boats.

A man sat beneath a tree reading a newspaper.

Every page was blank.

Yet he smiled after turning each one.

Two strangers played chess.

Neither seemed interested in winning.

No one hurried.

No one checked a phone.

No one argued.

For the first time in years...

Vivin felt completely still.

---

Then he stopped.

Across the road...

Parked beside a quiet café...

Was the car.

The exact model.

The exact colour.

The exact wheels.

His dream car.

He slowly walked toward it.

His fingers brushed against the hood.

Cold metal.

Real.

He smiled without realizing it.

"You like it?"

The teenager asked.

"I've wanted this car since I was twenty-five."

"Then maybe..."

"It has been waiting for you too."

Before Vivin could reply...

The café door opened.

A man stepped outside.

He wasn't old.

He wasn't young.

There was something timeless about him.

He looked at Vivin.

Then at the car.

"Going somewhere?"

Vivin blinked.

"Do I know you?"

The man smiled.

"Not yet."

He walked to the driver's side and opened the door.

"Come."

Vivin looked at the teenager.

The teenager simply nodded.

"As I said..."

"I've been waiting."

Vivin climbed into the passenger seat.

The engine started without a sound.

The car rolled forward onto a road that seemed to stretch beyond the horizon.

Neither side of the road had an end.

After several minutes, the driver finally spoke.

"Everyone who comes here arrives carrying a question."

Vivin looked out the window.

"And the answer?"

The driver smiled without taking his eyes off the road.

"When you leave..."

"It won't be the same question."

The road stretched endlessly ahead.

And for the first time...

Vivin wasn't sure he wanted to go back.
